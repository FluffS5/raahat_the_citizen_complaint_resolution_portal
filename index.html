Be heard because you’re right, not because you’re viral.

Raahat is a citizen complaint platform that combines the accountability of a formal ticketing system with the visibility of a public feed, so a complaint is resolved on need, not on follower count.

The problem we started from

Raahat grows out of the research paper “Equity or Virality? A Comparative Analysis of Formal Ticketing Systems and Social Media Platforms (Twitter/X) for Citizen Complaint Resolution in E-Governance” (mixed methods, survey of 44 respondents plus CPGRAMS and literature data). The paper is an unpublished study, shared here for reference. View research paper (PDF)

56.8%

believe viral complaints are more likely to get resolved, the strongest perception in the whole survey.

72.7%

have never filed on a formal portal, even though 100% use the internet daily. Awareness, not access, is the barrier.

50%

of portal users waited more than a week for a response, and 16.7% never heard back.

13.6%

say neither channel works, a trust deficit that points to a hybrid model.

Formal ticketing (e.g. CPGRAMS)

  • Equitable, trackable, accountable
  • Slow, low awareness, hard to file
  • Each complaint handled separately, with no grouping by locality

Social media (Twitter/X)

  • Fast, easy, public pressure
  • Rewards influence and virality
  • No guarantee of real resolution

The paper’s conclusion: neither channel alone is satisfactory. Raahat is an attempt at the integrated, hybrid system it recommends.

How Raahat works

One platform where filing, visibility and accountability live together.

1 · File a complaint

A structured ticket goes to the right authority, so the formal trail is never lost.

2 · Share it publicly

Post about it in a social-style feed with the relevant authorities tagged for visibility.

3 · Neighbours join in

Citizens filing a similar issue see matching posts and can back the existing one. The original complainant’s name stays private.

4 · Officials respond in public

Authorities post work-completed or delay updates in the feed, so progress is visible to everyone.

Core features

Public feed with official updates

A Twitter-like feed where officials post completion or delay notices against each complaint.

Upvote the cause

Residents who see the problem daily can upvote to show support without filing their own complaint.

Locality clustering

Multiple complaints about the same local issue are grouped and handled together, fixing a gap found in CPGRAMS. Severity is weighed alongside clustering.

Duplicate prevention

New filers are shown matching posts and asked whether it’s the same complaint before a duplicate is created.

Transparent ticket status

Detailed, visible statuses, ending not with an officer closing the ticket but with the citizen confirming the fix or reopening it.

Prototype scope

This demo shows the concept and the flow. Some things are deliberately out for now.

In this prototype

Filing, public feed, upvotes, status flow, and clustering demonstrated on a sample dataset.

Future scope

Aadhaar-linked accounts (to prevent duplicate logins), live real-time clustering, and automatic address resolution once authorities are onboarded.

Left out for now

Downvotes, public comments, and naming individual officers in posts.
